02Streaming & Digital

The money with no paper trail, tracked anyway.

The streaming buy never touches a public file, so we catch it on the glass, price it as a floor, and read it service by service.

How a streaming number is made

  1. Caught on the glass

    Streaming creative is captured on the CTV screen in measured homes, never read off a platform feed.

  2. Priced as a floor

    Detected delivery at calibrated rates: it reads as at least this much, and it rises only as coverage rises.

  3. Read service by service

    The split lands at app grain, the read the pitch and the plan actually need.

Why the modeled number deserves weight

  • It starts from observed viewing. Every estimate is anchored to ads actually caught on the glass in homes where viewing is measured, never to a survey projected outward from a few thousand diaries.
  • One home is counted once. A device graph resolves the TV, the streaming stick and the set-top box to a single household before anything is counted.
  • The dollars are calibrated on real invoices. Rates come from media we placed ourselves, used with those clients' written consent, priced at the conservative end so the translation errs low.
  • The discipline is the defense. Modeled prints estimated, partial prints as a floor, and an estimate is never summed into a posted total.

The full spine, drawn step by step with the honest detail, is on the methodology page, along with how a recalibration restates history.

03FCC rate intelligence

What a spot costs, from the public file.

Station-level political rates read off public FCC filings: what stations charged, by daypart and week, flight over flight. The rate conversation with a rep changes when you arrive holding the file. And when the buyer arrives holding it, so are you: the same file is how a station defends its rate.

What subscribers pull

  • Station rate history by daypart and week, FCC-verified where a filing exists
  • Flight-over-flight rate movement in a market as election pressure builds
  • The competitive buy's actual clearances beside the rate card you were quoted
  • Candidate and outside-group orders read separately: inside the lowest-unit-rate window candidates pay the protected rate for the class of time they buy; outside groups pay market rate all year.

Partnership announcements

Aug 10, 2026 As the signals campaigns rely on decay, KPI and CivicScience bet on declared data

Kinetiq Political Insights and CivicScience, the real-time consumer-insights company, announced a data partnership that brings CivicScience's declared-intent polling directly into KPI's political data layer: more than 10,000 always-on questions gathering over 1 million answers a day across 165 million U.S. consumer profiles.

The problem it answers is quiet and expensive. As third-party cookies and mobile ad IDs fade, political targeting increasingly runs on modeled-on-modeled data: lookalike audiences trained on other models, built from segments that were fresh a cycle or two ago. By some industry estimates, a lookalike model sheds 30 to 60 percent of its lift within 90 days of being built. In a midterm that KPI projects will reach $10.4 billion in ad spending, campaigns are paying premium rates against decaying data, and often cannot see the decay happening. CivicScience offers the opposite of a model: people, on the record, right now, refreshed daily.

"Political targeting has quietly become a game of telephone. We build a model off another model, off a segment that was fresh two years ago, and by Election Day we are aiming at a ghost," said Adam Wise, Chief Innovation Officer at Kinetiq Political Insights. "CivicScience breaks that loop. Our rule at KPI has always been simple: don't model the audience, find it. This is how we find it."

"Every day, millions of Americans answer our questions in the moment they are forming an opinion, not weeks later in a callback. That is the freshest signal in the market, and it is declared, not inferred," said John Dick, Founder & CEO of CivicScience.

Both firms are clear about the boundary: declared data is a fresher input, not a claim about any single ad's effect.
