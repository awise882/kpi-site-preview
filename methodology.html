How to read our numbers

Five instruments ride every figure we publish.

Each one exists because a specific error is expensive: the ways a number goes wrong, and the marks that stop it.

Open the five instruments

Instrument 01 · Through-date

Every figure names the date it stands on.

The posted-through date is the last date on which delivery is confirmed. A report run on Aug 12 does not make an order posted through Aug 2 into delivered weight; at or before the date the money ran, after it the money is reserved or projected. A single race can hold orders posted through different dates, so the published date is the earliest in the set, never the most flattering one.

Prevents Mixing what happened with what is projected.

Latency is stated per lane, and the lag is printed

Lanes do not refresh at the same speed, so coverage and cadence are published lane by lane on the lane-by-lane coverage, never under one "real time" badge that would be false in at least one lane. The lag itself is on the figure: every number carries the date it is posted through and the date it was refreshed, and the days between them are that lane's lag on that read, checkable on any export. Streaming settlement runs on its own varying cadence; the paired dates are the check there too. Ask for the current gap in a lane you buy, or the market you sell, and we will give it to you in days.

Instrument 02 · Aired versus booked

Money that ran and money on the books are never one number.

Aired weight is what the electorate has already seen. Booked-forward weight is a reservation that can be moved, added to or cancelled, and it is the part that says how much runway is left. The split happens at the through-date, in the figure above, and it never blends.

Prevents A forward reservation reading as delivered weight, which is how a campaign concludes it has been outspent in a week nobody has run yet.

Instrument 03 · The floor

Where detection is partial, we publish the floor and call it a floor.

A floor is built only from airings we detected; nothing is filled in behind it. It reads as at least this much, and the detected volume rises only when coverage improves. The dollar translation prices measured delivered impressions at rates calibrated against invoices from media we placed ourselves, used with those clients' written consent and priced at the conservative end of the calibrated range, so the translation errs low. The figure carries the word estimated wherever it prints, and a rate recalibration publishes as a versioned restatement, never a silent move. We walk through the calibration on a call.

Prevents A model presented as a measurement. Detected impressions multiplied by a market rate can be a reasonable estimate, and it is still not a detection.

The firewall

That calibration is the one place client media touches the competitive product: rates only, in aggregate, with written consent, never plans, placements or delivery. Everywhere else the firewall holds: subscriber queries, saved races, saved audiences, delivery reports and exports are never visible to or used by any media-buying engagement other than your own, and no buyer's plan feeds the competitive surfaces.

The covered digital source list, published

Every digital share we publish is share of measured digital sources, never of all of digital, and this is the current list: ad-supported streaming apps on smart TVs, premium and free ad-supported, and YouTube delivery on TV screens. Dynamically inserted ads inside live-TV streaming services are contracted and in validation; they join the list when they start printing numbers, not before. Mobile, desktop and walled-garden social sit outside this instrument. The list expands as measurement expands, and this page gets a version note when it does.

How a streaming airing is detected

Streaming is addressable, so detection samples from measured households: the capture happens on the CTV glass, in homes we measure, not from a platform feed. Narrowly targeted buys under-detect more than broad ones, one reason the streaming dollar prints as a floor. Extrapolating from measured households to the household universe is one reason it prints as estimated; that is not modeled fill, which would mean inventing platforms, creatives or weeks that were never detected.

Where the delivered-impressions measurement runs

Delivered-impressions measurement covers the top 58 U.S. media markets by household count; races outside that footprint show booked and aired figures only, and the screen says so. Monitoring of broadcast, local cable and radio runs in all 210 media markets regardless; the two footprints are different things and every figure names the one it stands on.

Instrument 04 · Insufficient signal

Where coverage cannot support a number, the cell says so instead of printing a zero.

A zero is a claim: it asserts that no advertising ran. When we cannot support that claim, the cell carries an insufficient-signal state, and it stays out of totals, rankings and share denominators rather than quietly counting as nothing, with the exclusion disclosed.

Prevents Absence of detection being read as absence of advertising, the most expensive error in this category.

Instrument 05 · The declared answer

A polling read is declared, dated and weighted — and its method has a named owner.

Answers come from people who chose to answer, so the raw stream is not a random sample and we do not present it as one. Reads are weighted toward representativeness under CivicScience's methodology, which governs recruitment, weighting and quality control; we name the owner rather than restating their method, and we will put their documentation in front of you. An audience builds on a stated recency window, and the questions that build it are named, so the audience is auditable before a dollar moves.

Prevents A convenience stream being read as a random sample, and a fresh-sounding audience quietly built on stale answers.

How we count

A dollar becomes a number in six steps, and the figure names which ones applied.

Each step either holds a distinction the next step could blur, or hands the figure a label it has to carry to the page.

Open the six steps, the weighting spine and the contest grid

  1. An airing is detected

    From the transmission itself as it runs, never inferred from a posted schedule or a survey. Detection is the atomic event; everything published is an aggregation of detections.

  2. The airing is matched to a market and a channel

    A geography and a lane, not a national bucket: the level a buy is actually placed at.

  3. Airings are grouped to the order that placed them

    Order grain is what makes the aired-and-booked split possible; without it the only thing left to divide is a total.

  4. Spend is FCC-verified where the filing exists

    Where no filing covers the lane, the figure says what it does stand on, and never borrows the credibility of a filing that was never made.

    Streaming is addressable, and narrow buys under-detect more than broad ones in measured households — one more reason a streaming dollar prints as a minimum, never a total.

  5. Orders carry their own through-dates

    The published through-date is the earliest in the set, the one the whole figure can stand behind.

  6. The published figure states which instruments apply

    Through-date, the aired and booked split, any floor, any insufficient-signal cell. If a figure leaves the product, the marks leave with it.

What we do not do at any step

We do not fill an undetected week from a neighboring week. We do not spread a cycle-level estimate across markets and present the result at market grain. We do not let a projection inherit the through-date of a posted figure it sits next to.

Where you can see this working

Every export and every app screen carries the marks. Open any figure in the platform and the marks are on the same screen as the number, not in a methods appendix nobody opens.

Restatement policy

History moves when better source data lands, so every figure carries the version of the data it was computed on.

A filing covering a week in June can post in August; when it does, the June number was wrong and is now right. We restate, we version, and we say so: the version tells you whether the race changed or the source data did.

What triggers one

A late filing on a published week, a correction on an existing order, or coverage improving where we stood on a floor.

What we publish

The restated figure, the prior figure, both data versions, and one line naming which weeks and lanes moved. Announced, never slipped in.

Why this is a feature

A series that never restates is either fully filed at publication or it is not looking. Ours restates because it keeps checking.

Accuracy claims

An accuracy figure that does not name its estimator, its panel and its universe is not an accuracy figure.

This category advertises accuracy percentages more often than it defines them. Our rule applies to us first: any accuracy claim we make names what was computed, what it was graded against, and the scope it was measured over.

Open the accuracy rule

Swipe the table sideways to see every column.

A claim must name Why a buyer needs it What its absence hides
The estimator Which quantity was computed. Spend, points, impressions and reach are four different estimators; accuracy on one says nothing about the others. An accuracy figure earned on a total being read as accuracy on the line item you buy.
The panel or source it was graded against Something has to stand in for truth, and a filed invoice, a station affidavit and a viewership panel are not interchangeable standards. A comparison against a source with the same blind spot as the estimate, which grades agreement rather than accuracy.
The universe Which markets, lanes, dates and races were in scope. Accuracy in the top markets on broadcast is a claim about the easiest part of the problem. A result measured on the well-covered slice and quoted as if it held down-ballot and off broadcast.
The grain it was graded at Per market and per week is the grain a buy is placed at, so it is the grain worth grading. One cycle-level projection compared with one cycle-level actual, a single observation dressed as a rate.

A validation is only as good as the pool it ran on. Where a result comes from a pool chosen after the fact, that has to be disclosed and discounted going forward.

The rule, stated plainly

We do not publish a headline accuracy percentage that grades a different quantity than the one a buyer uses. Where we have a validation, it names the estimator, the panel, the universe and the grain, and we will walk through it on a call rather than compress it into a badge. Where we have not run one for a lane, we say that too.

Glossary

The terms, as our outputs use them.

Several of these words are used loosely across the category. If a vendor's definition differs from one of these, that difference is usually where the two numbers diverge.

Open the glossary

Swipe the table sideways to see every column.

Term What it means in a KPI output
Airing One transmission of one ad on one channel at one time. The atomic unit every published figure aggregates.
Detection Identification of an airing from the transmission itself as it runs, never inferred from a schedule, a media plan or a panel diary.
Order grain Spend attached to the placement contract behind the airings: a dollar traces to a station, a flight and a buyer, not to a market average.
Posted through The last date on which delivery is confirmed. At or before it, the money ran; after it, the money is reserved or projected.
Filed through The last date covered by the public filings behind an FCC-verified figure. Filings post on a lag, so it can trail the posted-through date on the same screen; the gap is the lane's lag.
Settled through The last date the streaming measurement cycle is complete for that figure. Later detections accrue as a restatement, never a silent change.
Booked forward Money committed on a station's books for dates after the through-date, in the lanes where orders exist: broadcast, local cable, radio. Real and cancellable, so it reports as runway, never as weight. Streaming and digital carry no order document, so no forward book is published there.
Committed Aired plus booked forward, with the two parts always visible on their own lines, never blended away.
Points (GRPs) Gross rating points: one point is one percent of the stated target universe reached once. Points are household points on the market universe unless the export names a demo in the column header, and they are population weighted when combined, never simply summed across markets.
Impression One ad delivery to one device or screen, before any deduplication. A delivery count, never a stand-in for households or people reached.
Reach Households reached at least once inside the measured footprint. Reported in households, never persons, because the measurement is of a household's screens.
Frequency Impressions served per household reached inside the measured footprint. A household is not a person, and we never restate a household frequency as a per-person number.
Deduplication Counting a household once when it saw the ad on both linear and streaming. Observed viewing plus modeled linkage, so it is an estimate with a range, quoted as a shape to move budget against rather than to the last household.
Lane One distribution channel treated as its own measurement unit: broadcast, local cable, radio, streaming or digital. Sourcing, coverage and latency differ by lane, so every figure names the lanes it covers.
Streaming lane vs digital lane The streaming lane is detected creative on named ad-supported streaming apps, with dollars an estimated minimum built from those detections. The digital lane covers the measured sources beyond those named apps, always estimated and never a minimum, with the covered-source list published on this page.
Live-TV streaming Live-TV streaming apps, where a linear feed can masquerade as streaming, print as pending until we can tell the difference. Pending is a published state, not a zero.
Floor The number we can stand behind where detection is partial, built only from detected airings with no modeled fill. It reads as at least this much, and it rises when coverage improves.
Estimated The label on modeled spending: dollars modeled from detected creative activity and measured delivery, published where no order and no public price signal exists. Every modeled figure carries the word, and an estimate is never merged into a posted total.
Estimated minimum The label on a weighted streaming figure: dollars built from delivery measured in metered homes, weighted to the household universe, calibrated to err low. Prints light with ≈, reads as at least this much, and never joins a posted total. Distinct from a floor, which stops before any weighting.
Projected The label on forward inference: any figure past the last order on file. Useful for planning, never evidence, never merged into an aired total, and distinct from estimated, which labels modeled dollars for activity that was detected.
Insufficient signal A published state meaning coverage in that cell cannot support a number. It appears instead of a zero, and the cell is held out of totals and rankings with the exclusion disclosed.
Restatement A versioned, announced revision of an already published historical figure after better source data lands, reconcilable against the prior read.
Share of race money One advertiser's spend as a percentage of all spend in that race, counted the same way: aired, committed, or detected. That count, its window and its lanes travel with the number, because the denominator moves when any of them changes.
Definitional advantage The weight one side put behind an argument before the other side answered it on the same subject, as a share of total weight on that argument. It measures who set the terms of the race, not who spent more.
Declared audience An audience built from the answers people gave to a named question, not from a model of people who resemble them. The question that defines it is visible, so you can judge what it means.
Deal ID The identifier a curated audience and its inventory are delivered under, so the audience activates in the buying seat you already use.
Undercard Races below the top of the ticket. Local cable and radio frequently carry the majority of the weight there, which is why a broadcast-only view can miss most of an undercard buy.

Definitions are the ones enforced in the products and exports. If an output uses a term that is not on this list and the meaning is not obvious, tell us and we will either define it here or stop using it.
