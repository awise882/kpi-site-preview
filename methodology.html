Why this page exists

Every figure we publish names its basis, so you can check it.

Competitive figures get used to move money. A buyer shifts weight between markets, a manager decides whether to answer a spot, a committee re-cuts a reserve. Every one of those calls depends on knowing which part of a total already ran and which part is a reservation someone can still cancel.

So we publish the basis with the figure rather than in a footnote or a sales call. Four instruments do that work. They appear in the products, in the exports, in the weekly memo and in anything a KPI analyst hands you. Where our coverage is thin, they are the reason you can tell.

The grammar

Four instruments carry the basis, and every figure we publish uses them.

Each one exists because a specific error is expensive. Read them as the four ways a competitive number goes wrong, and the four marks that stop it.

Every figure names the date it is posted through

Instrument 02 · Aired versus booked

Money that ran and money on the books forward are never one number

Both are real and they answer different questions. Aired weight is what the electorate has already seen. Booked-forward weight is a reservation that can be moved, added to or cancelled, and it is the part that tells you how much runway is left.

As printed

$14.2M

Aired $9.6M  ·  Booked forward $4.6M

Split at the through-date, never blended Booked cancellable, stated as such

Prevents A forward reservation reading as delivered weight, which is how a campaign concludes it has been outspent in a week nobody has run yet.

Instrument 03 · The floor

Where detection is partial we publish the floor and call it a floor

A floor is built only from airings we detected. Nothing is filled in behind it. It reads as at least this much, and the detected volume rises only when coverage improves. The dollar translation prices measured delivered impressions at rates calibrated against invoices from media we placed ourselves, used with those clients' written consent, priced at the conservative end of the calibrated rate range, so the translation errs low. That calibration is the one place client media touches the competitive product, and it is taken with written consent: rates only and in aggregate, never plans, placements or delivery. The firewall holds everywhere else: subscriber queries, saved races, saved audiences, delivery reports and exports are never visible to or used by any media-buying engagement other than your own, and no buyer's plan feeds the competitive surfaces. The figure carries the word estimated wherever it prints, and a rate recalibration publishes as a versioned restatement, never a silent move. We walk through the calibration on a call.

Instrument 04 · Insufficient signal

Where coverage is inadequate, the cell says so instead of printing a zero

A zero is a claim: it asserts that no advertising ran. When we cannot support that claim, the cell carries an insufficient-signal state, and it stays out of totals, rankings and share-of-race denominators rather than quietly counting as nothing.

From detection to publication

A dollar becomes a number in six steps, and the figure names which ones applied.

The sequence matters more than any single stage. Each step either holds a distinction the next step could blur, or it hands the figure a label it has to carry to the page.

  1. An airing is detected

    The spot is identified from the transmission itself as it runs, not inferred from a posted schedule or reconstructed from a survey. Detection is the atomic event, and everything published is an aggregation of detections.

  2. The airing is matched to a market and a channel

    It lands in a geography and a lane rather than a national bucket, which is what allows a figure to be read at the level a buy is actually placed.

  3. Airings are grouped to the order that placed them

    Order grain is what makes the aired and booked split possible. Without it there is no principled way to say where delivery ends, because the only thing left to divide is a total.

  4. Spend is attached on an FCC-verified basis where the filing exists

    Where a public filing covers the order, the dollars stand on it. Where no filing exists for that lane, the figure states the basis it does stand on, and does not borrow the credibility of a filing that was never made.

  5. Orders carry their own through-dates

    A single race can hold orders posted through different dates. The published through-date is the one the whole figure can stand behind, which means it is the earliest one in the set, never the most flattering one.

  6. The published figure states which of the four instruments apply

    Through-date, aired and booked split, whether a floor is in play, and whether any cell is insufficient signal. If a figure leaves the product, those marks leave with it.

What we do not do at any step

We do not fill an undetected week from a neighboring week. We do not spread a cycle-level estimate across markets and present the result at market grain. We do not let a projection inherit the through-date of a posted figure it sits next to.

The covered digital source list, published

Every digital share we publish is share of measured digital sources, never of all of digital, and this is the current list: ad-supported streaming apps on smart TVs, premium and free ad-supported, and YouTube delivery on TV screens. Dynamically inserted ads inside live-TV streaming services are contracted and in validation, and they join the list when they start printing numbers, not before. Mobile, desktop and walled-garden social sit outside this instrument. The list expands as measurement expands, and this page gets a version note when it does.

How a streaming airing is detected

Streaming is addressable, so detection samples from measured households: the capture happens on the CTV glass, in homes we measure, not from a platform feed. Narrowly targeted buys under-detect more than broad ones, which is one reason the streaming dollar figure prints as a floor. The floor rises only as coverage rises, never from modeled fill. Extrapolating from measured households to the household universe is one reason the dollar prints as estimated; that is not modeled fill, which would mean inventing platforms, creatives or weeks that were never detected.

Where the delivered-impressions measurement runs

Delivered-impressions measurement covers the top 58 U.S. media markets by household count. Races outside that footprint show booked and aired figures only, and the screen says so rather than substituting a guess. Monitoring of broadcast, local cable and radio runs in all 210 media markets regardless; the two footprints are different things and every figure names the one it stands on.

Latency is stated per lane, and the lag is printed

Lanes do not refresh at the same speed, and a single "real time" badge across all of them would be false in at least one. Coverage and cadence are published lane by lane on the coverage summary, including where they are slower than we would like.

The lag is on the figure itself. Every number carries the date it is posted through and the date it was refreshed, and the days between those two dates are that lane's lag on that read. It is a measured gap you can check on any export, which is why we publish it that way instead of quoting one service level that would go stale the week a station files late. Streaming settlement runs on its own cadence, and that cadence varies; the paired dates on every figure are the check there too. Ask for the current gap in a lane you buy, or in the market you sell, and we will give it to you in days.

Where you can see this working

Every export and every app screen carries the marks. Open any figure in the platform and the basis is on the same screen as the number, not in a methods appendix nobody opens.

Restatement policy

History moves when better source data lands, so every figure carries the version of the basis it was computed on.

Filings arrive after the flight. A file covering a week in June can post in August, and when it does, the June number was wrong and is now right. We restate, we version, and we say so.

Versioning is what makes two reads reconcilable. If a figure pulled in July and a figure pulled in August disagree, the version tells you whether the race changed or the basis did. Without it, the two reads become an argument between two people holding screenshots.

What triggers one

A filing posts for a week already published, a correction lands on an existing order, or coverage improves in a lane where we had been standing on a floor.

What we publish

The restated figure, the prior figure, the basis version of each, and one line naming which weeks and which lanes moved. A restatement is announced, not slipped in.

Why this is a feature

A series that never restates is either fully filed at the moment of publication or it is not looking. Ours restates because it keeps checking, and the version is how you tell the difference.

Accuracy claims

An accuracy figure that does not name its estimator, its panel and its universe is not an accuracy figure.

This category advertises accuracy percentages more often than it defines them. Our rule applies to us first: any accuracy claim we make names what was computed, what it was graded against, and the scope it was measured over.

Swipe the table sideways to see every column.

A claim must name Why a buyer needs it What its absence hides
The estimator Which quantity was computed. Spend, points, impressions and reach are four different estimators, and being accurate on one says nothing about the others. An accuracy figure earned on a total being read as accuracy on the line item you buy.
The panel or source it was graded against Something has to stand in for truth. A filed invoice, a station affidavit and a viewership panel are not interchangeable standards. A comparison against a source with the same blind spot as the estimate, which grades agreement rather than accuracy.
The universe Which markets, lanes, dates and races were in scope. Accuracy in the top markets on broadcast is a claim about the easiest part of the problem. A result measured on the well-covered slice and quoted as if it held down-ballot and off broadcast.
The grain it was graded at Per market and per week is the grain a buy is placed at. That is the grain worth grading. One cycle-level projection compared with one cycle-level actual, which is a single observation dressed as a rate.

A validation is only as good as the pool it was run on. Where a result comes from a pool chosen after the fact, that has to be disclosed and discounted going forward.

The rule, stated plainly

We do not publish a headline accuracy percentage that grades a different quantity than the one a buyer uses. Where we have a validation, it names the estimator, the panel, the universe and the grain, and we will walk through it on a call rather than compress it into a badge. Where we have not run one for a lane, we say that too.

Glossary

These are the terms our outputs use, defined the way we use them.

Several of these words are used loosely across the category. If a vendor's definition differs from one of these, that difference is usually where the two numbers diverge.

Swipe the table sideways to see every column.

Term What it means in a KPI output
Airing One transmission of one ad on one channel at one time. The atomic unit of the dataset, and the thing every published figure is an aggregation of.
Detection Identification of an airing from the transmission itself, by matching the spot as it runs, rather than inferring it from a posted schedule, a media plan or a panel diary.
Order grain Spend attached to the specific placement contract behind the airings, so a dollar traces to a station, a flight and a buyer instead of to a market-level average.
Posted through The last date on which delivery is confirmed for that figure. At or before it, the money ran. After it, the money is reserved or projected.
Filed through The last date covered by the public filings behind an FCC-verified figure. Filings post on a lag, so a filed-through date can trail the posted-through date on the same screen; the gap is the lane's lag on that read.
Settled through The last date the streaming measurement cycle is complete for that figure. Later detections can still accrue for dates after it, and when they do the figure moves as a restatement, never a silent change.
Booked forward Money committed on a station's books for dates after the through-date, in the broadcast, local cable and radio lanes where a filing exists. It is real and it is cancellable, so it is reported as runway rather than as weight. Streaming and digital carry no order document, so no forward book is published for those lanes.
Committed Aired plus booked ahead: money that has already run plus money on a station's books for dates after the through-date. Where a figure reads committed, the aired and booked-forward parts stay visible on their own lines, never blended away.
Points (GRPs) Gross rating points. One point is one percent of the stated target universe reached once. Points are household points on the market universe unless the export names a demo in the column header. Demo-specific points are available by request, and the demo travels in the header wherever it applies. Points are specific to a market's universe, so they are population weighted when combined and never simply summed across markets.
Impression One ad delivery to one device or screen, before any deduplication. Impressions are a delivery count, so they never stand in for how many households or people were reached.
Reach Households reached at least once inside the measured footprint. It is reported in households and never in persons, because the measurement is of a household's screens.
Frequency Impressions served per household reached inside the measured footprint. A household is not a person, and we never restate a household frequency as a per-person number.
Deduplication Counting a household once when it saw the ad on both linear and streaming. It is built on observed viewing and modeled linkage, which makes it an estimate with a range around it, and it is quoted as a shape you can move budget against rather than to the last household.
Lane One distribution channel treated as its own measurement unit: broadcast, local cable, radio, streaming or digital. Basis, coverage and latency differ by lane, so every figure names the lanes it covers.
Streaming lane vs digital lane The streaming lane is detected creative on named ad-supported streaming apps, with dollars an estimated minimum built from those detections. The digital lane covers the measured sources beyond those named apps, always estimated and never a minimum, with the covered-source list published on this page.
Floor The number we can stand behind where detection is partial, built only from detected airings with no modeled fill. It reads as at least this much, and it rises when coverage improves.
Estimated The label on modeled spending. A dollar figure modeled from detected creative activity and measured delivery, published where no order and no public price signal exists. Every modeled figure carries the word estimated, an estimate is never merged into a posted total, and a disclosure and an estimate are never silently blended.
Projected The label on forward inference: any figure past the last order on file, an expectation about weeks that have no document behind them yet. Useful for planning, never evidence, and never merged into an aired total. Distinct from estimated, which labels modeled dollars for activity that was detected.
Insufficient signal A published state meaning coverage in that cell cannot support a number. It appears instead of a zero, and the cell is held out of totals and rankings with the exclusion disclosed.
Restatement A revision of an already published historical figure after better source data lands, most often a filing that posts late. Restatements are versioned, announced and reconcilable against the prior read.
Share of race money One advertiser's spend as a percentage of all spend in that race on the same stated basis: aired, committed, or detected. The basis, the window and the lanes travel with the number, because the denominator travels with it and moves when any of them changes.
Definitional advantage The weight one side put behind an argument before the other side answered it on the same subject, expressed as a share of total weight on that argument. It measures who set the terms of the race rather than who spent more in total.
Declared audience An audience built from the answers people gave to a named question rather than from a model of people who resemble them. The question that defines it is visible, so you can judge whether it means what you need it to mean.
Deal ID The identifier a curated audience and its inventory are delivered under, so the audience activates in the buying seat you already use.
Undercard Races below the top of the ticket. Local cable and radio frequently carry the majority of the weight there, which is why a broadcast-only view can miss most of an undercard buy.

Definitions are the ones enforced in the products and exports. If an output uses a term that is not on this list and the meaning is not obvious, tell us and we will either define it here or stop using it.
